# Off-Catalog editorial rules

## Headlines and decks

- Lead with the watch, brand, reference, person or event. Follow with the specific reason it deserves a story. Aim for 6–14 words and roughly 70 characters; clarity matters more than an exact count.
- Use title case in the editor. The site handles uppercase display. A colon can separate subject and angle. Do not use em dashes or presentation variants.
- Write direct, specific headlines. Avoid empty hooks such as “Pay Attention,” “Here's What Actually Happened,” “Just Dropped” and generic luxury language.
- Keep Henry's point of view. Do not add new facts, availability claims or market conclusions just to strengthen a headline. Keep predictions labeled as predictions and retain the original publication date.
- Write one concise subtitle that adds useful context instead of repeating the title. Check number claims against the body. Use the same headline in article metadata and social previews unless a deliberately shorter SEO title is needed.
- Editing an existing headline must preserve its slug, canonical URL, article body, original publication date and assigned watch artwork.

## Journal presentation

- The newest published story leads the landing page automatically, sorted by publication date. Use its opening cover photograph, with the first inline image as fallback when no cover exists. Never substitute a watch cutout for the photographic hero.
- Dim the lead photograph and center the headline, short deck and read link. Maintain readable contrast and a usable layout without an image.
- Follow with a spacious three-column story grid on desktop, two columns on tablet and one on mobile. Keep the approved watch cutouts large, contained and uncropped. Artwork assignments must use stable article slugs, not changing headlines.
- Every article uses a wide opening photograph and a white reading panel that overlaps its lower edge. Keep body text comfortably sized, with clear section breaks, full-width inline photographs and readable captions.
- Keep the Dialed By H navigation, Off-Catalog name, original photography and existing watch artwork. Use editorial serif headlines within the journal, with brand typography for navigation and the masthead.
- Verify desktop and mobile overflow, image loading, long headlines, empty/error states, keyboard focus, reduced motion and image-less articles before shipping.

## Publishing boundaries

- Verify watch facts against original sources. Preserve the difference between editorial coverage and products available to source.
- Unavailable releases must use informational CTAs, without sourcing, allocation or DM-to-buy promises.
- A layout or headline update does not authorize a subscriber email. Sending requires its own explicit instruction.

## Related website copy

- Apply the same measured, specific tone to sourcing prompts. Prefer “Personal Watch Sourcing,” “Submit a Sourcing Request” and “Message Henry” over punchy sales hooks.
- Explain the next step clearly: review availability and pricing for the requested watch. Do not promise a quote, availability or access before checking.
- Avoid unsupported claims about transaction volume or off-site inventory, such as “Most of what I move never reaches the site.”
- Use readable sentence-case body copy and a neutral dismissal such as “Continue Browsing.”
